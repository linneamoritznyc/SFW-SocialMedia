## Caption

A landmark paper from the 1000 Farms Initiative is out, and it measures regenerative agriculture the way farmers practice it: as a whole system.
Ecdysis Foundation researchers placed working farms across North America on a scale from most conventional to most regenerative. The most regenerative farms held 39% more total carbon in their soil and 77% more total fungi. Soil microbes, insects, plants and birds were all more abundant, and that biodiversity tracked closely with how much carbon each field stored.
Regenerative yields matched national averages, and net profit per acre was similar to conventional farms.
The finding we'd point every consultant to: farms using just one regenerative practice looked the same as conventional farms. The outcomes came from the full system, living soil included.
Congratulations to Dr. Jonathan Lundgren and the Ecdysis team.
