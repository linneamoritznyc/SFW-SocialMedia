# Three captions, rewritten against the Messaging House

Companion to the voice diagnosis. The Messaging House v1.0 (August 2026, board
and leadership review) is the reference. Section numbers below are its numbers.

The problem these rewrites are answering: across 23 finished captions in this
repository the median caption is exactly five paragraphs, 39 percent of
sentences are under nine words, 30 sentences open with "That is", and 17 of
those are "That is the real / actual / whole ___". The house has a name for
what that costs us. Section 7.4 asks for "wonder grounded in rigor" and for
copy that is "respectful of their expertise, we never talk down to the people
who feed us". A sentence that ends every paragraph by telling the reader which
part mattered is talking down. We kept the rigor and lost the wonder.

Each rewrite below is paired with what it replaces, and each says which rule it
is breaking on purpose.

## 1. Blunt. The microbes line

Replaces the caption in `drafts/2026-10-23-bunch-of-microbes.md`. That version
runs six paragraphs and spends four of them explaining a nine word joke, then
closes on "Same arrangement. Different scale. Nobody in either system asked to
be part of it." Three fragments in a row, in the position where the current
house style always puts them.

Pillar: GROW IT, reaching for the roof. Rung: Documented for the meeting, and
deliberately unnumbered for the biology, per the note in the draft about the
ten to one ratio.

> In the end, aren't we all just a bunch of microbes?
>
> Somebody said it to close our all-team meeting and everyone laughed, and then nobody could find anything wrong with it. You carry a microbial population in numbers comparable to your own cells. It breaks down what you cannot, and it holds surfaces that something worse would otherwise take. So does the top six inches of a healthy field.
>
> Save it for the next time someone asks what you do.
>
> #SoilFoodWeb #LivingSoil #SoilBiology #SoilMicrobiome #SoilHealth #Microscopy

Three paragraphs instead of six. One sentence does the work that four did. No
fragment, no "That is", and the parallel between a body and a field is stated
once and then left alone, because a joke that gets explained is not one.

## 2. Long. The rice and wheat rotation

Replaces the caption for post 2 in `variants/india/captions.md`. The original
is not bad. It is the same length and shape as the microbes joke and the UMass
funding announcement, which is the objection. This one earns its length.

Pillar: PROVE IT, backed by TEACH IT. Rung: mostly Documented mechanism. The
recovery order still needs Carla's sign off, and the three to ten year range
stays labelled as the Foundation's own stated range, not a measured result.

> Rice, then wheat, then rice again, for forty years. The biology under a field like that does not fail all at once, which is part of why it is so easy to miss.
>
> Flooding does the first half of the damage. Water fills the pore space and pushes the oxygen out, so the root zone sits anaerobic for weeks at a time. Tillage does the second half, and it does something quite specific. A fungal network is made of threads, so cutting it is less like pruning a plant and more like cutting a cable.
>
> Bacteria come through both. They are single cells, they tolerate low oxygen, and they divide in hours. Fungi come through neither. They need oxygen, they exist only as a connected network, and rebuilding one takes seasons rather than days. So forty years of that rotation does not leave you with dead soil. It leaves you with bacterial soil, which still looks busy under a microscope and behaves quite differently in the field.
>
> What goes with the fungi is what a grower feels first: the glues and threads that bind soil crumbs together, so rain soaks in instead of running off, and the slow release of nitrogen that suits a crop rather than the fast nitrate flush that suits the weeds competing with it.
>
> Recovery has an order too, and the order is the reason people give up on it. Bacteria return first. Then the protozoa that graze them and release nitrogen where roots can reach. Then beneficial nematodes. Fungi last, and slowest. Which means your first season back rarely looks like anything at all, and by the second most people have already decided it did not work.
>
> Left to itself, that takes somewhere between three and ten years, depending how far the soil has gone. That range is ours, from teaching this, not a measurement from one site. The alternative is to grow the missing organisms somewhere else and put them back, which is what compost and extracts are for.
>
> We are in India from 19 to 30 October and the events are open.
>
> #SoilFoodWeb #SoilBiology #NaturalFarming #LivingSoil #SoilHealth #Agroecology #RegenerativeAgriculture

Seven paragraphs, but the sentences run long and the reader is trusted to hold
a thought across a clause. The one line that behaves like an aphorism, "it
leaves you with bacterial soil", is load bearing: it is the finding, not a
decoration on the finding.

## 3. Quote led. Sandra's trial

Replaces option C in `variants/captions.md`. The original is already the
quietest of the three options there, and it is still narrated in our voice
rather than hers.

Pillar: PRACTICE IT. Rung: Documented, which under section 7.1 is the rung
where numbers are allowed, in the "this farm, this season, this result" form.
Consent and the tag handle are still outstanding.

> "Soil biology rebuilds the ground before it rebuilds the yield."
>
> Sandra Niggemeyer wrote that at the end of a season of butternut squash on Texas clay and sand that had been fallow for twenty years. She ran three plots on the same field: biologically complete compost tilled into one, teas and extracts through the season on another, water only on the third.
>
> Organic matter gained over the season: 46 percent on the compost plot, 14 on the control, 1 on the teas. Harvest: 35 ounces, 30 and 19. One field, one season, and she is the first person to say so.
>
> The result she did not expect is the one she kept. The tea plot, which barely moved the organic matter at all, carried the fewest squash bugs. The control carried the most. Occupying leaf and root surfaces is a different job from building soil structure, and one unreplicated season cannot separate them.
>
> Hail killed her first planting. Rodents took the second. Johnson grass ran the beds by June. She wrote all of it down, which is why there are numbers at all.
>
> [SLOT: two or three sentences in Sandra's own words, taken from her report. Ask her which paragraph she would want quoted. This is the point of the piece and it is currently missing.]
>
> Every other Thursday we publish what a field trial found.
>
> #SoilFoodWeb #LivingSoil #SoilBiology #RegenerativeFarming #SoilHealth #SoilFoodWebFoundation

**The rule this one breaks on purpose.** "Hail killed her first planting.
Rodents took the second. Johnson grass ran the beds by June." is exactly the
three beat fragment rhythm the diagnosis objects to. It stays. Three real
events in the order they happened is what that rhythm is actually for. The
objection was never to the device, it was to the device appearing in every
caption whether or not it had anything to carry.

**And the thing this one cannot fix.** A genuinely quote led caption needs more
of Sandra's words than this repository holds. We have one sentence of hers.
Inventing a second would be the worst thing on the page, so the slot stays
visible until somebody asks her.

## Six places the drafts and the Messaging House disagree

Found while reading the house against the repository. All six are small and all
six are the kind of thing that gets noticed in a board review.

1. **The evidence ladder does not match.** Section 7.1 sets three rungs:
   Documented, Testing, Believed. The drafts use eight labels invented on the
   way through: Documented, Established, NEEDS CHECK, AMBIGUOUS, UNVERIFIED,
   UNRESOLVED, PENDING VERIFICATION, and "Established, but write it carefully".
   Most of what is tagged Established is really Documented. The open questions
   are not a rung at all, they are a blocker, and should be a separate field.
   **Fix:** three rungs only, plus a `blocked_on:` line.

2. **The pillars do not match.** The house has four: PROVE IT, TEACH IT,
   PRACTICE IT, GROW IT, each mapped to a 2027 to 2029 strategic priority. The
   drafts sort themselves into five of our own invention: Community Voice,
   Events and Programs, Science Explained, The Bigger Picture, Take Action.
   These are content categories, not message architecture, and section 2 is
   explicit that every public statement should be recognizable as one of the
   four. **Fix:** keep our categories as a format label, add the house pillar.

3. **"Soil Regenerators" appears nowhere.** Section 7.3 names it as the
   community identity, to be capitalized, used warmly, and "always shown
   peopled, never as an abstraction". The drafts say "Soil Food Web
   practitioners" seven times instead. The India attendees post is the exact
   case the rule was written for.

4. **The em dash rule conflicts with the house itself.** `CLAUDE.md` bans them.
   The Messaging House uses them heavily, including inside the master narrative
   and the ten and thirty word pitches, which are meant to be quoted verbatim.
   Somebody has to rule on this, because right now we cannot reproduce our own
   boilerplate without breaking our own style rule. My recommendation is that
   the ban stays for social copy, where the dash is the single most recognizable
   tell of generated text, and the house keeps its dashes in board and press
   material where the register is different.

5. **"Living soil is the solution" appears in no draft.** It is the roof. If
   section 2's test is real, that every public statement should be recognizable
   as holding it up, then the core idea should surface in the copy sometimes,
   not only in the architecture.

6. **The house's own best lines are not in the phrase bank.** "Authority fades;
   evidence compounds." "Science that can't be checked isn't science."
   "Measurable, teachable, repeatable." Those are three sentences somebody here
   wrote, which is the only entry criterion `sop/phrase-bank.md` has. They
   belong in it.

## What to change in the SOP, if these land

The caption structure in `sop/` currently specifies hook on line one, science in
lines two to five, the implication, one call to action, five to eight hashtags.
That is what produced the isometry. The factual guardrails are what protect us
and they should stay exactly as they are: sources named, consent in writing, no
invented figures, no unattributed percentages, every dated item dated.

Drop the paragraph choreography. Keep the ladder.
