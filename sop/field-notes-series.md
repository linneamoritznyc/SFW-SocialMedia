# Field Notes

The graduate celebration series. Written 17 September 2026.

Every graduate of the consultant path finishes with a field trial on real ground:
a hypothesis, plots, a season, and a written report. Carla posts each graduation
in the company chat with a summary. Until now nobody turned those into posts.

This series exists because a field trial hands us the four things generic soil
science content never has: a named person, a named place, a named season and
numbers somebody actually measured. That clears the evidence bar in a way
"one teaspoon of soil contains more organisms than there are people on Earth"
does not, however true that is.

---

## The promise

**We publish what the trial found, including when the results disappoint.**

This is the whole series. A graduate whose compost plot beat the control gets a
post. A graduate whose plots came out level gets a post that says so. A graduate
who lost two plantings to hail and rodents gets a post that says that too,
because a reader who has lost a planting is the reader we most want.

The moment we only publish the wins, the numbers stop meaning anything and the
series is just congratulation with a chart on it.

Thursday is already the Community Voice or Science slot in the strategy
calendar, so this series fills a slot that exists rather than adding one. Every
other Thursday leaves the alternate Thursday free for other community content and
keeps the series from outrunning the supply of graduations.

**The stacking rule.** Field Notes is a Community Voice post, not a promotion.
It never runs in the same week as a second promotional post, and the caption
never carries an enrolment call. The trial is the content. If a reader wants to
know how a person gets to run one, the profile link is where that lives.

## The seven slide carousel

Slide by slide, and the visual direction in `variants/` that fits each one.

| # | Slide | What is on it | Direction |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **The question** | What the trial set out to answer, on a photograph or the specimen layout. Name, place, season | 4 or 7 |
| 2 | **The ground** | What state the land was in before. Soil type, history, what was grown | 4 |
| 3 | **The design** | The plots and what each one got. Always name the control | 7 |
| 4 | **The soil result** | The biological result. Bars from zero, one measure | 3 |
| 5 | **The crop result** | The yield, in the units it was measured in | 3 |
| 6 | **What went wrong** | Weather, pests, weeds, anything that hit all plots. Not optional | 6 |
| 7 | **Her conclusion** | The graduate's own sentence, and the single call to action | 5 |

Slide 6 is the one that will get argued about. Keep it. It is the slide that
makes slides 4 and 5 believable, and it is the slide a working grower stops on.

**Caption structure**, which is the house structure and not a new one:

1. **Hook**, line one. Specific, and a number or a counterintuitive claim.
2. **The science**, lines two to five. The mechanism in plain language. Name
   organisms. No jargon without a definition.
3. **The implication.** What it means for the reader's own ground.
4. **One call to action.** Never more than one.
5. **Five to eight hashtags.** That is a ceiling, not a target.

**LinkedIn adaptation.** The seven slide texts become seven paragraphs, in the
same order, with the hook as the opening line. Roughly 15 minutes. It is the
right platform for this series because a field trial is a case study, and case
studies are what that audience opens.

## The six swappable fields

Every graphic in `variants/` carries these six and only these six, marked in the
markup with `data-field`. Keeping them identical across directions is what makes
the series rebuildable by somebody who did not design it.

| Field | Sandra Niggemeyer, 2026 | Source |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `hero-number` | 46% | The headline finding |
| `place` | Van, Texas | Carla's announcement |
| `headshot` | not yet supplied | The graduate, after consent |
| `test-question` | Can soil biology rebuild ground that sat fallow for twenty years? | The report's hypothesis |
| `soil-result` | 46% compost, 14% control, 1% teas and extracts | The report |
| `crop-result` | 35 oz compost, 30 oz extracts, 19 oz control | The report |

Two identity fields travel with them: `name` and `season`.

**Every number carries a source line naming the graduate's report and the year.**
No unattributed percentages, anywhere, ever. This is a standing brand rule and it
matters more here than anywhere else on the account, because these are the only
numbers we publish that are not from a paper.

## Internal course names never appear in public copy

Write the name out. The acronyms are internal shorthand and several of them mean
something else entirely to a reader outside the organisation.

| Never write | Write instead |
| :-- | :-- |
| FC, FC1 to FC4 | Foundation Courses |
| AP, AP Course | Advanced Programs |
| PDC | Permaculture Design Certificate |
| IER | Introduction to Ecosystem Restoration |
| CTP, CLP, GTP | Retired names. Do not use at all, internally or publicly |
| SSW, SSR | Soil Sponge Workshop |
| CIC | Write the course name out |
| IN, AW and the rest of the filing codes | Never public. They are file naming, not names |

**AP is the sharpest one.** A reader in the United States reads "AP Course" as
Advanced Placement, the college credit programme, which implies an accreditation
the Foundation does not claim and is actively moving away from.

**Do not write "certified" either.** The organisation is pulling back from acting
as a certifying body, no outside body recognises the consultant credential, and
"certified" is already on the banned list in the copy deck. Say what is true:
she completed her field trial, she is a graduate, she ran the trial on her own
ground.

Also banned across the account, and easy to slip into on a celebration post:
"nature's operating system", "world's foremost", any income claim, any yield
claim that is not this graduate's own measured result, and em dashes.

## The pipeline

1. **Carla** emails the graduate's information and links the field trial report.
   *Open action: Carla to copy Linnea directly, not only Stephanie.*
2. **Read the report, not the summary.** Slides 3, 6 and 7 need things a summary
   will not have: the plot design, the failures, and a sentence in her own words.
3. **Extract the six fields.** Write the source line at the same time, not after.
4. **Carla science-checks the takeaway.** She is the check on whether the finding
   as stated is what the trial actually showed.
5. **Ask for consent.** See below. This gates everything.
6. **Draft into the shared document.** Caption, seven slide texts, LinkedIn
   adaptation, and the asset paths.
7. **Stephanie approves** copy and image, or sends it back.
8. **Build in Canva** from the approved direction, then **schedule in Later.**

## Consent

**Consent is required and is not currently being obtained.** That is the state as
of 8 September 2026, and it is the single biggest risk in this series. Nothing
here runs without it.

Message the graduate before anything is designed:

- Introduce yourself as being on the social team.
- Say what has been made and where it will appear.
- Ask permission to post it.
- Ask for the handle they want tagged.
- Ask separately about their photograph, because agreeing to have a trial written
  up is not the same as agreeing to have a face posted.

No stand-in faces. No mentor photographs in a graduate's slot. If consent does
not come back, the quote card direction still runs with attribution the graduate
has agreed to, or the post does not run.

Anyone else recognisable in a supplied photograph has to have agreed too.

## What a good Field Notes post is not

- Not a congratulation with a statistic attached.
- Not a percentage without the control number beside it.
- Not a yield claim scaled up from a small plot. Sandra's harvest was 35 ounces
  against 19. That is a real difference and about two pounds against one, and
  turning it into "84 percent more squash" would be dishonest about the sample.
- Not an enrolment pitch. Stephanie owns promotional content. This is not it.
- Not a post that hides the season. Hail, rodents and Johnson grass hit all three
  of Sandra's plots and belong on slide 6.
