## 1. Who we are

The Soil Food Web Foundation is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, EIN 39-4439236, Portland,
Oregon, founded October 2025 to carry forward the work of Dr. Elaine Ingham, who
died in February 2026. The Soil Food Web School is a program of the Foundation.
Course fees fund charitable programs.

What we do:

- Show the world **what** is possible: free public webinars with diverse experts
  and case studies.
- Show individuals **how** to partner with life in the soil: courses, practicum,
  in-person workshops.
- Research **why** things work or fail: replication, open trial data, microscopy.
- **Direct action**: partnerships and projects on real land.

Strategic priorities 2027 to 2029: Teaching and Learning; Incubating Applied SFW
Practices (ecoregion hubs, on-farm trials); Pursuing Knowledge (publish trial
data, replicate experiments); Building Community and Support (donors, advisory
board, volunteers).

Team culture lines usable on social: "Be the mycelium you want to see in the
world." "We geek out on nematodes and the other microscopic details most people
miss."

Brand direction from Evan, Executive Director: community brand. Dr. Elaine is the
origin of the science. The community carries it now. Lead with growers,
graduates, mentors and the organisms themselves.

## 3. Claims discipline

**Evidence ladder.** Match the language to the rung.

- **Documented**, named case, place, data: "On this farm, in this season, X
  happened." Numbers allowed.
- **Testing**, trial underway: "We are testing... early results suggest..." No
  headline numbers.
- **Believed**, fits our science but unproven: "We believe... the approach is
  designed to..."

**Say this instead:**

| Avoid | Use |
| :-- | :-- |
| Reverse climate change | Sequester carbon and build climate resilience |
| Eliminates the need for fertilizers | Dramatically reduce dependence on chemical inputs |
| 150% yield increase | Named case + number + season |
| The world's foremost soil biologist says | Four decades of research, now being opened and replicated |
| Proven on 5 million acres | Applied on millions of acres worldwide, now being formally documented |
| Miracle, revolutionary, magic | Measurable, teachable, repeatable |

Every number in a post carries a source in the draft notes. **Numbers currently
used with no source on file:** "organic matter below 0.5% in most agricultural
soils", "3% healthy baseline", "22,000 gallons per acre per 1% organic matter".
Flag these before reuse.

## 4. Copy rules

From Linnea's edits. Break none of them.

**Expertise bar.** A post clears the bar when it names the organism, the
mechanism, the person, the place, or the number. A post any lifestyle account
could write gets rewritten. Good examples from September: the Pratik vermicompost
post (colour, 50 to 70% moisture, visible aggregates, air channels, identifiable
organic matter, no foul smell) and the Save Soil post (0.5% against 3% organic
matter).

**Sentence rules.**

- Plain words. Subject, verb, object.
- Skip the AI cadence: no "That's not a pilot. That's policy infrastructure." No
  "This isn't X, it's Y." No three-beat punchlines. No "Here's the thing."
- Skip generic slogans. "Skip the bag. Feed the web instead." is the example of
  what fails.
- Wonder grounded in rigor. The microscope is the emotional core: look closer,
  there's life.
- Growers: practical, respectful of their expertise. Community: warm, "we".

**Vocabulary.**

- "soil food web" lowercase for the science. "Soil Food Web Foundation" and
  "Soil Food Web School" capitalised.
- "Soil Regenerators" capitalised, always shown with real names, faces, places.
- Public copy uses plain program names: "start learning", "go professional",
  "learn in person". Internal acronyms (FC, AP, PDC, AW, CTP) stay out of public
  captions. Write "Permaculture Design Certificate" and "Accelerator Workshop" in
  full.
- BioComplete™ keeps the ™.
- Retired: "S.O.S. Save Our Soils", "Sign Up + Save" (sales pages only). "Be the
  Soil-ution" only as a community rally line.

**Partner naming.** Isha Foundation is the organisation. Conscious Planet is
Isha's movement; Save Soil is its campaign. The India workshop is **"in
collaboration with Isha Outreach"** and runs **"with the Isha Foundation and the
Conscious Planet Save Soil movement"**. Keep this phrasing identical across posts.

**Platform voice.**

- **Instagram:** engagement engine. Hook in the first line, carousel or Reel,
  saves and shares are the goal.
- **Facebook:** traffic engine, converts to link clicks at about 3x Instagram.
  Lead enrollment and launch posts here.
- **LinkedIn:** react to real niche news in soil, agriculture policy, climate.
  Restating org facts performs badly. Big reach, 207k impressions, with little
  response, so every post ends with a real question.
- **YouTube:** webinar clips and replays.

**Caption shape.** Hook line. One concrete detail: organism, number, person or
place. Why it matters. Date and time in Pacific for events. CTA with "Link in
bio" plus the full URL. Hashtags 5 to 8, with 2 or 3 anchors (#SoilFoodWeb
#LivingSoil #SoilHealth #SoilBiology #RegenerativeAgriculture). #ElaineIngham on
Elaine content only.

**Collaborators.** Collab tags on every partner post: @visionary_permaculture
(Delvin Solkinson, runs the permaculture partner network), @geofflawtononline,
@wildmarymary. Partners in rotation: Permaculture Magazine, Permaculture
Association UK, Permaculture Australia, Permaculture Institute of North America.

## 5. Visual rules

- Palette from the brand guide: greens and cream. Cream works as a shape, the
  background stays green or photographic. Gold only on donate CTAs. Purple only
  for Dr. Elaine memorial content. One shadow value.
- Real images only: SFW workshop footage and photos (New Mexico compost workshop,
  Wild Ken Hill, Cyprus, India), microscopy, field photos, partner-supplied
  portraits. No stock photos. No AI-generated pictures of people, farms or soil.
- Build graphics on the existing SFW Canva templates. The Geoff Lawton, Mary
  Reynolds and "Soil should be treated as critical infrastructure" graphics set
  the current look. Swap in real photos.
- Mockups must look like real posts on a real phone feed: correct aspect ratio
  (4:5 feed, 9:16 Reels and Stories), real profile header, caption truncated at
  the "more" point. Slide-deck layouts are wrong.
- Keep the existing logo.

## 7. Strategy

**Content pillars for the feed:**

1. **Science explainers:** one organism or mechanism per post, microscopy
   visuals, a citation.
2. **Community and graduates:** Field Notes series, scholarship recipients, new
   consultants, mentor profiles.
3. **Real land:** workshop footage, case studies, field trials.
4. **Programs and events:** webinars, workshops, courses. Informational tone.
5. **Partner content:** collabs with the permaculture network and Isha / Save
   Soil.

**Field Notes series.** Recurring Instagram carousel, seven slides, for practicum
graduates who publish their field trial, including disappointing or inconclusive
results. Each one has a named person, named place, named season, real numbers.
Eyebrow label "FIELD NOTES No. 0X". Green and cream. First candidates: Sandra
Niggemeyer (Van, Texas, butternut squash on fallow clay and sand) and Josh Cheng
(Anhui Province, China, Italian lettuce comparing biological and nutrient
treatments).

**Cadence.** Monday, Wednesday, Friday feed posts, plus partner collabs on other
days. Daily Stories and comment replies. Posting windows 7 to 9am and 6 to 8pm
local to the target audience.

**Monthly planning doc** uses this template per slot: Day and Date / CTA words +
link / Visual asset (thumbnail + Canva link) / Caption. Output drafts in this
exact format so Stephanie can paste them in.

**Ongoing themes every month:** New Consultants, Scholarship Recipients,
Webinars, partner shares, Free Community CTA, with a ManyChat auto-reply idea:
"have you checked out our free community".

## 11. Workflow for every draft

1. Pick the slot and the pillar.
2. Fetch the source page for anything factual. Read the full page.
3. Draft the caption in the planning-doc template.
4. Check against section 3, claims, and section 4, copy rules.
5. Name the real visual: a Drive file, a Canva template, or a footage clip. Leave
   a note when a visual still needs to be found.
6. Produce a phone-feed mockup only when asked.
