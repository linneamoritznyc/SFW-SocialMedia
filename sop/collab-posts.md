# Instagram collaborative posts

Standing practice. Written 17 September 2026 from the all-team meeting notes of
the same date.

## Why this is a rule and not a suggestion

From the meeting: the PDC launch used collaborative posts with guests and with a
permaculture magazine, and Stephanie reported **a large jump in reach and
followers.** The notes call it "the only marketing tactic named all meeting that
measurably worked, and nobody proposed doing more of it."

Set against the rest of the picture: ad spend was nearly eliminated across the
whole of the last year, so every view the account gets is earned. A collab is the
only lever we have that costs nothing and puts a post in front of a second
audience.

**One caveat on the size of the effect.** The notes record "a large jump in reach
and followers". They do not carry a number, a baseline or a comparison period.
So this SOP says the tactic worked and is worth repeating. It does not claim a
multiplier, and neither should any deck. If Stephanie has the figures, they
belong here.

## What a collab actually does

An Instagram collaborative post appears on both accounts, in both grids, with one
shared like and comment count. The invited account accepts and it goes live on
their profile too. It is not a tag and it is not a repost.

## Which post types always get one

| Series | Collab partner | Who asks |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Field Notes**, every graduate post | The graduate's own account | Whoever drafts the post, in the same message as the consent request |
| **India**, every post | Isha Outreach, RYSS, or Return to Earth, whichever the post is about | Evan or Kavi, because they hold the relationship |
| **Guest webinars**, every one | The guest, and their organisation | Whoever booked the guest. This is the tactic that worked at the PDC launch |
| **Scholarship recipients** | The recipient's account | In the consent email, as one more question |
| **Press and partner coverage** | The publication | Whoever holds the contact |

Everything else is optional. A collab request to someone with no reason to say
yes is a small cost to the relationship, so do not send one for the sake of it.

## When to request

**Before the post is scheduled, not after.** An invitation sent after publishing
means the post has already spent its first hour on one account, which is the hour
that matters.

Order of operations:

1. Consent for the content, and the handle, in the same message.
2. Draft goes to Stephanie, approved as normal.
3. Collab invitation sent from the scheduling step, to the handle they gave.
4. Wait for acceptance before the post goes live. If it is not accepted by the
   scheduled time, publish without it. Do not chase twice.

## How to ask

Inside the consent message, not as a separate email. One sentence:

> If you are happy to, we would like to post this as a collaboration so it appears
> on your profile as well as ours. If you send me your handle I will send the
> invitation, and you can accept or ignore it.

"Accept or ignore it" is deliberate. It gives a silent way out.

## When someone has no account

Common, and not a problem.

- **Post without the collab.** Name them in the caption as you would anyway.
- **Do not create an account for them,** and do not tag a namesake.
- **Ask whether their organisation has one.** A farm, a co-op, a restoration
  project or an employer will often accept, and the reach is comparable.
- **Ask whether they want to be tagged at all.** Some graduates would rather be
  named and not linked. That is a complete answer.

## What never goes in a collab

- Anything with an unconfirmed likeness on it.
- Anything where the partner has not seen the final copy. A collab puts our words
  in their grid, and a partner discovering a claim they disagree with after
  publication is a relationship problem, not a social media problem.
- Promotional posts with a price in them, unless the partner is a co-host and has
  agreed to that specific post.

## Where this gets recorded

Every draft's front matter carries a `collab` field: the handle, or `none` with
the reason. A draft with an empty `collab` field is not finished.
