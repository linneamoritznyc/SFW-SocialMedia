## The consent email

Send before anything is designed. One message per recipient, their name at the
top, sent by a person rather than an address nobody reads.

> Subject: Your photo, and one sentence, for our social media
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> I am [YOUR NAME], I work on social media at the Soil Food Web Foundation.
>
> We would like to post about the scholarships awarded in June, and we would like to include you. That would mean your photograph, your first and last name, and the country you are in appearing on our Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn accounts. The posts are public, so anyone can see them and they can be shared onward.
>
> Two questions.
>
> First, are you happy for us to do that? If you would rather we did not, please just reply "no thanks". Nothing happens as a result. It does not affect your scholarship, your place on the course, or anything else, and you do not need to give a reason. If you prefer, we can use your first name only, or your country without your name.
>
> Second, if you are happy to be included: would you write one sentence about what you intend to do with the training? We would quote it exactly as you write it, next to your photo. It does not need to be polished. If you would rather not be quoted, that is fine too and we will just use your name and country.
>
> If you have a social handle you would like tagged, send that as well.
>
> One last thing: if there is a photograph of yourself you would prefer we use, please attach it. Otherwise we will use the one from your application.
>
> Thank you either way,
> [YOUR NAME]

**Why it is written this way.** It says exactly where the photograph goes, it
gives a one-word way out, it says out loud that declining costs them nothing, and
it offers the middle options (first name only, country without name, photo but no
quote) so that "no" is not the only alternative to "yes". People being asked for
a photograph by an organisation that funded them are not in a neutral position,
and the email has to account for that.

## Voice checks done

- No "empowering", no "transforming lives", no "these incredible individuals".
- The photographs carry the feeling. The words are flat and specific.
- Places are named.
- Nothing is said on any recipient's behalf. Every quoted sentence is theirs,
  exactly as written, or the slide runs without a quote.
- The Foundation is not the subject of the headline.
- One call to action per piece.
- No em dashes, no internal course acronyms, no "certified".

## One design flag

The collage sits on a soft pink ground, and **pink is not in the brand token
set.** The six slides around it are built in the brand greens and cream, so the
collage will read as an imported artefact rather than part of the system.

That is probably the right call anyway. It is the best visual anyone in the
organisation has made, re-grounding it risks killing what makes it work, and one
slide out of seven carrying a different ground reads as a document rather than a
mistake. But it is a deliberate choice and Stephanie should make it rather than
discover it.

**Route everything through Stephanie, and do not schedule until every consent is in.**
