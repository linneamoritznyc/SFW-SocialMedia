## The treatment

**Total: 48 seconds. Vertical, 1080x1920. Burned-in captions throughout, because
most of the feed watches muted.**

### Beat 1, the hook. 0 to 2 seconds.

The first two seconds decide everything. Do not open on a logo, a title card, or
the speaker introducing themselves.

Open on the **strongest surviving-green-against-burnt-ground frame in the file.**

On-screen text, one line, large:

> The fire went through here.

Audio: ambient, or the speaker's first concrete sentence. Not an intro.

### Beat 2, the stakes. 2 to 10 seconds.

Widest shot of the burn. What was lost.

On-screen text:

> [SLOT: what burned, in the speaker's own words]

### Beat 3, the turn. 10 to 20 seconds.

The garden, or the part that came through.

On-screen text:

> [SLOT: the speaker's line about what survived]

This is the moment the viewer decides whether to stay. If the file has a shot of
the boundary, burnt on one side and green on the other, it goes here and nowhere
else.

### Beat 4, the mechanism. 20 to 38 seconds.

The reason the reel is ours rather than a local news clip. Keep the speaker's
audio if they explain it; use text over B-roll if they do not.

Three lines, one at a time:

> Soil that holds together holds water.
>
> Bacteria and fungi make the glues that bind soil into crumbs. Fungal threads
> weave those crumbs into bigger ones.
>
> A soil like that behaves like a sponge. A compacted one sheds water and burns
> dry.

That is mechanism 3 from the Foundation's own science page, so it needs no new
source. **Do not claim the biology caused the survival** unless the speaker does,
and even then attribute it to them. One garden is not evidence of a mechanism.

### Beat 5, the close. 38 to 48 seconds.

Back to the surviving planting. Let it sit for two seconds with no text before
the last card.

> [SLOT: the speaker's closing line]

Then:

> Follow for what we find in the soil.
