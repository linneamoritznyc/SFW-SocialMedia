# The formulas are visible

## Why this is not a feature announcement

The notes call it "a scientific integrity claim with a product behind it". That
is the frame, and it is the only frame that makes this interesting to anyone who
is not already a user.

A soil analysis tool that shows its arithmetic is making a statement about what
kind of organisation publishes it. **It is the post that earns the right to be
believed on every number we put out afterwards**, including every Field Notes
figure. Run it before the Field Notes series builds up, not after.

**Do not lead with the product name.** Lead with the principle. The tool is how
the principle is kept, not the subject.

## The treatment

**45 seconds, vertical, screen recording, burned-in captions.**

### 0 to 2 seconds, the hook

Cursor already moving. No intro, no logo.

> Most soil analysis software will not show you how it got the number.

### 2 to 12 seconds, the problem

Screen: a result. A ratio, a biomass figure, whatever the tool outputs.

> You count what you see down a microscope. You type it in. A number comes out.
>
> And then you are asked to trust it.

### 12 to 30 seconds, the reveal

**This is the whole reel.** Click through to the formula view and let it sit.
Scroll it slowly. Do not cut away. The viewer needs long enough to register that
this is the actual arithmetic and not a marketing screen.

> Every calculation in ours is visible.

Then, as it scrolls:

> You can follow it from your own count to the number it produces, and check it.

### 30 to 40 seconds, why it matters

Stay on screen, or cut to a microscope.

> If you cannot see the formula, you cannot argue with the result.
>
> And if you cannot argue with it, it is not science. It is just a reading.

### 40 to 45 seconds, the close

> Check our arithmetic.

## Caption

> Most soil analysis software will not show you how it got the number. You count what you see down the microscope, you type it in, a result comes out, and you are asked to trust it.
>
> Every calculation in ours is now visible. You can follow it from your own count through to the figure it produces, and check it.
>
> That sounds like a small feature. It is not. If you cannot see the formula, you cannot argue with the result, and a number you cannot argue with is not a scientific finding. It is a reading from a box.
>
> We publish soil results from real trials on real ground, and we are going to publish a lot more of them. This is the part that makes those worth reading.
>
> Check our arithmetic.
>
> #SoilFoodWeb #SoilBiology #Microscopy #SoilHealth #OpenScience #LivingSoil

## What has to be captured

- **One clean screen recording at vertical crop**, or a desktop capture that can
  be cropped to 1080x1920 without losing the formula text. Test the text size at
  390px wide before recording the whole thing.
- **A real dataset, not a demo one.** Use a genuine sample if the person recording
  is comfortable with the numbers being public, which they will be.
- **Casey records it.** He holds the tool and will know which screen actually
  shows the arithmetic best.

**One thing not to do:** do not show a user's data without asking them. A sample
result belongs to whoever collected it.

## The honest caveat

The notes also record that this tool "would randomly delete user data" at launch
about a year ago. Nobody needs that in the post, and nobody should pretend it did
not happen if asked. If a comment raises it, the answer is that it did, it was
fixed, and the tool now has tutorials, regular fixes and a feedback path. That
answer is better than silence and it is in the notes.
