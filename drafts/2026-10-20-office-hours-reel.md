# Office hours, as a reel

From the notes: students arrive with one question and stay the full hour. Three
times a week. Carla runs them. It is described as "the strongest engagement
signal in the organisation" and it is "described nowhere public".

## The treatment

**60 seconds, vertical, burned-in captions.**

### 0 to 2 seconds, the hook

Open mid-answer. Not on a title card, not on Carla introducing herself.

On screen:

> She came with one question. She stayed the whole hour.

### 2 to 12 seconds, the question

One real student, asking one real question. Unedited, whatever they actually
asked. The more specific and more mundane, the better: a fungal-to-bacterial
ratio that looks wrong, a pile that will not heat, something they saw at 400x and
cannot identify.

> [SLOT: the student's question, in their words]

### 12 to 40 seconds, the answer

Carla answering. This is the body of the reel and it should be the least edited
part of it.

If she goes to a microscope or a diagram, cut to what she is looking at and stay
there. The thing that makes this footage worth anything is that it is a real
answer to a real problem, not a piece to camera.

> [SLOT: Carla's answer, cut to the single clearest 25 seconds]

### 40 to 52 seconds, the turn

Wide shot of the call, several faces in the grid.

On screen, one line at a time:

> Three times a week.
>
> One question each, usually.
>
> Nobody leaves at the fifteen minute mark.

### 52 to 60 seconds, the close

> This is what the advanced practicum actually buys.
>
> Follow for what students actually ask.

## What footage has to be captured

**Nothing usable exists.** No recording of an office hour is in `assets/` and
none is named in the meeting notes.

To make this reel somebody has to:

1. **Record one full session**, with the host's screen and the gallery view.
   Zoom's local recording is enough. Separate audio tracks per speaker if the
   platform offers it, because the student's question and Carla's answer need to
   be cut independently.
2. **Get a clean microscope capture** from the same session if she goes to one.
   Screen recording of shared video is usually too soft to read.
3. **Shoot thirty seconds of Carla** not on a call: at her own microscope, or
   looking at a sample. The reel needs one image that is not a video grid.
4. **Keep the raw file.** Sixty seconds is one reel. A recorded hour is a term's
   worth of them.

## What consent is required, and from whom

This is the part that has to be right, because students are paying customers in a
small cohort and they will recognise each other.

**Before the session:**

- **Announce it in advance**, not at the start of the call. Email the cohort a few
  days ahead: this session will be recorded, clips may be used publicly, here is
  how to opt out.
- **Offer a real opt-out that costs nothing.** Attend with camera off and we do
  not record you. Or skip that session and come to the next one, which runs twice
  more that week. If someone would have to miss help they paid for in order to
  avoid being filmed, the opt-out is not real.

**At the start of the session:**

- **Say it again on the call,** before recording starts, and name who is being
  recorded.

**After, and this is the one people skip:**

- **The student whose question is used gives separate written consent**, after
  seeing the actual cut. Agreeing to be in a recording is not agreeing to be the
  subject of a sixty second clip on a public account.
- **Their name and face are two separate permissions.** They may want the question
  used and their face blurred, or their voice used and their name off it.
- **Carla consents too**, as an employee being used in marketing, in writing.

**Anyone who does not consent comes out of the cut,** including from the gallery
view wide shot. That means the wide shot is the hardest frame to clear and should
probably be staged with volunteers rather than pulled from a live session.
