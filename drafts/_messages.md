## 1. Evan, about the Guardian piece

**Why.** The notes record it twice: *"The Guardian piece exists and has not been
published publicly yet. Evan put it in the company chat."* And separately, among
press and appearances: *"All of it exists and none of it is shown."*

A Guardian piece is the single highest-authority thing the Foundation has. It is
sitting in a chat window.

**Send to Evan, in the chat where he posted it:**

> Evan, the Guardian piece you dropped in here. Do you have a publication date, or
> is it already out and just not shared anywhere?
>
> Reason I ask: if it is live, it is the strongest third-party thing we have and
> it is currently visible to about twelve people. I would build a carousel around
> it and put it on the press page Allison and I are scoping.
>
> If it is not out yet, I will hold and you can tell me when.
>
> Either way, could you send the link rather than the screenshot?

**Short, and it asks one question.** He is stretched, and a long message gets read
later.

### The carousel to build once there is a date

Five slides, The Bigger Picture pillar.

1. **The pull quote.** Whatever the piece says about soil biology, set in EB
   Garamond, attributed to the Guardian with the date. Not a screenshot of the
   headline: a screenshot reads as a brag, a set quote reads as a source.
2. **What the piece is about**, in our words, fairly. If it is not mainly about
   the Foundation, say so on this slide rather than letting slide 1 imply it.
3. **The part they got right**, expanded with the mechanism. This is where we add
   something rather than reflecting.
4. **The part that needed more room.** Every general-audience piece on soil
   biology simplifies something. Naming what, generously, is the slide that makes
   this ours rather than a repost.
5. **Read it**, with the link.

**Do not run it as "we were in the Guardian".** Run it as "the Guardian covered
this, here is the biology underneath it". The first is a press release. The
second is a post.

## 2. Allison, about the field trial reports

**Why this is not a request.** The notes are unambiguous that she is already
there: *"Most notably: excited that AP graduates' field trial reports are being
promoted internally, and said she wants to help take those to market as case
studies."* And: *"Allison wants to take them to market. That is a two-person
project with an obvious home on the new site."*

The notes also recommend: *"Ask Allison to co-own the evidence layer of the site."*
So this message is written as two people discovering they are already doing the
same job, because that is what happened.

**Send to Allison:**

> Allison, you said in the meeting chat that you want to take the field trial
> reports to market as case studies. I have been building a social series out of
> exactly those and did not know you were on it, which is funny and also the whole
> problem the meeting was about.
>
> Here is what I have. Every other Thursday, one graduate's trial: what they
> tested, what the soil did, what the crop did, and what went wrong. It runs as a
> carousel and a LinkedIn piece, and it is called Field Notes. The first one is
> drafted off Sandra Niggemeyer's butternut squash trial in Texas.
>
> The thing that is missing is supply. I have one report. You have the pipeline.
>
> Could you send me ten reports this week? They do not need to be the best ten or
> the most flattering ten. I want a spread: different crops, different countries,
> and at least two where the result was disappointing, because the series only
> works if we publish those as well.
>
> What I would give back: each one becomes a social carousel, a LinkedIn post and
> a written case study in the same pass, so your case studies and my posts come
> out of one read of each report rather than two.
>
> And one thing I need from you that I cannot do alone: the graduates have to
> consent before any of this is public, and the notes say we are not currently
> collecting it. You know these people better than I do. Can we design that ask
> together?

**Why it is framed this way.** She volunteered three times in one meeting and
nobody picked it up. A request that arrives as "can you send me ten things" makes
her a supplier. This one makes her a co-owner, which is what the notes already
recommend and what she actually offered.
