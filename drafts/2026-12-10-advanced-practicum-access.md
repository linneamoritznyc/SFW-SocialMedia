# The advanced practicum post

## Two rules this post has to obey

**1. The acronym never appears.** Internally it is two letters. Publicly it is
"our advanced practicum", or better, it is described by what students do in it:
run a real field trial on real ground with a mentor, and publish what they find.

An American reader also reads those two letters as Advanced Placement, the
college credit programme, which implies an accreditation the Foundation does not
claim. That has already been flagged to Evan in the course catalogue review.

**2. This is the post where the Google Ad Grant risk lives.** The grant was
rejected because the copy read too commercial. So:

- **It explains what the course contains.** It does not sell an outcome.
- **It never leads with becoming a consultant.** The notes record that the
  organisation is actively unwinding the career framing, that Evan and the board
  are pulling back from acting as a certifying body, and that no income claims
  are made. A post that opens with "become a certified consultant" contradicts
  the organisation's own position as well as the grant criteria.
- **The word "certified" does not appear.**
- **One call to action, and it points at information, not at checkout.**

If the draft reads like a sales page on the second pass, cut the second half.

## The five slides

**1.** Some of what we teach is only open to students in the advanced practicum.
Here is what sits behind that door and why.

**2. A mentor, and no clock on them.** Mentor access is not metered by the hour.
Students are assigned a primary mentor and can reach the wider mentorship team
through scheduled office hours.

**3. Office hours, three times a week.** Carla runs them. Open once you have
started the first part of the practicum. Students arrive with one question and
stay the full hour. The notes call this the strongest engagement signal in the
organisation, and nobody outside knows it exists.

**4. A real field trial, on real ground.** Design a hypothesis, mark plots, run a
season, collect the data yourself, write it up, and defend the conclusion. It is
the capstone and it is why the door exists: it is a supervised research project,
not a video course, and supervision does not scale to everyone.

**5. And then we publish what you found.** Including the seasons that disappoint.
