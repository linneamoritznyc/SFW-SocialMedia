# Fall and winter soil prep

## The attribution I cannot complete

The anchor is meant to be a graduate who recommends incorporating biologically
complete compost in the fall rather than the spring, so the organisms have a
season to multiply before planting.

So the anchor is written as a marked slot below, and the post cannot run until

## The six slides

**1. The frame**
> The growing season is over. The soil season is not.
>
> Four things worth doing between the last harvest and the first hard frost.

**2. Get compost on now, not in spring**
> [SLOT: graduate name, place, year] ran her field trial and came out of it
> recommending the opposite of what most of us do: put biologically complete
> compost on in the fall.
>
> The reasoning is about time. Compost is not a fertiliser, it is an inoculant.
> What you are adding is organisms. Put them out in autumn and they have the
> whole cool, damp end of the year to spread through the soil, colonise residue
> and find their food. Put them out in spring and you are asking them to
> establish and deliver in the same eight weeks.

**3. Leave the residue. Do not tidy.**
> Every stalk and leaf you take off the bed is food you are removing from the
> organisms that build your soil.
>
> Bacteria and fungi break that material down, and it is their glues and their
> threads that bind soil particles into crumbs. Bare, swept soil loses that
> structure to rain impact and to freeze and thaw.
>
> Chop it, leave it, cover it.

**4. Get something living in the ground**
> A cover crop is not for the cover. It is for the roots.
>
> Living roots release sugars that feed the microbial community around them,
> all winter, in soil that would otherwise be idle. A mix does more than one
> species, because a monoculture cover feeds a monoculture biology.
>
> If it is too late to establish one where you are, mulch instead. Something on
> top is better than nothing.

**5. What happens under the snow**
> The biology does not die. It slows.
>
> Under snow the soil sits insulated and damp, and organisms keep working
> slowly through the residue you left them. In a dry, open winter with no snow
> cover the same soil freezes hard, dries out and loses more of them.
>
> Which is the real argument for mulch and cover in a climate that does not
> reliably get snow.

**6. The ask**
> Save this and work through it before your first frost.
>
> Whatever you get done in the next few weeks is what your soil has to work with
> until March.
