# The Spanish course post

## Why this is worth a post at all

From the notes: Allison Duck, who does marketing and communications, **did not
know the Spanish course existed.** The notes draw the obvious conclusion: "If
someone doing marketing and communications does not know about a translation,
nobody outside will find it."

That is the whole argument. This is not a launch. It is telling people about
something that already shipped.

## The acronym

The internal name is an acronym and it does not go in public copy. **Ask Elena
what the course is actually called in full** before this runs. She is named in
the notes as the person to ask about curriculum. The post below describes it by
what it teaches rather than by its name, which works either way.

## The post, in Spanish

> **Ya está en español.**
>
> Nuestro curso de compostaje biológicamente completo.

## Who reviews the translation

**I wrote this and I am not a native speaker. It needs a real reviewer before it
posts.** From the notes, the candidates are:

- **Gerald Ramirez.** Traditional agronomist, teaches, and would catch the
  technical vocabulary as well as the grammar. **He is the one I would ask.**
- **Isadora Schmidt.** Brazilian, lives near Barcelona. Portuguese first
  language, Spanish daily. Good on fluency, and see the Portuguese draft.
- **Elena Kalli.** Named as the curriculum person, so she should check the course
  description regardless of who checks the language.

Ask for two things, not one: **is the Spanish right, and is the soil vocabulary
the vocabulary a Spanish-speaking grower actually uses.** Those are different
questions and the second is the one that makes it credible.
