# Myth 1: "Plant beans and they will feed your tomatoes"

## Why this myth and not another

The Foundation holds two myth banks, both inside `SFW Social Ideas_.docx` in the
Drive social media folder: twenty on soil regeneration and twenty on
permaculture. Neither has been posted.

Most of the soil regeneration bank is aimed at farmers and at industry arguments
("you can't feed the world this way", "it's too expensive to transition"). Those
are the wrong fight for a home gardening audience.

The one with the widest circulation among home gardeners is not in the numbered
list at all. It is in Stephanie's braindump above it: **the nitrogen fixing
companion planting myth.** Three Sisters guides, companion planting charts and
"interplant beans with heavy feeders" advice repeat it constantly, and the
Foundation already has a written rebuttal with a real mechanism in it.

**One caution on the source.** Both banks are headed "Chat GPT top 20 myths".
The myth statements are machine generated and the rebuttals are partly annotated
by the team. They are a starting list, not a cited reference. Every rebuttal used
in a post gets checked before it runs. Stephanie wrote "(check?)" against the
nodule mechanism herself, and that check has not happened.

## The seven slides

**1. The myth, stated plainly**
> "Plant beans next to your tomatoes. They fix nitrogen and feed them."

**2. What people do because of it**
> So the beans go in between the tomatoes. The nitrogen never arrives, the
> tomatoes stay hungry, and the bag of fertiliser comes out anyway.

**3. What is actually happening, part one**
> Legumes do fix nitrogen, but not on their own and not out of generosity.
> Rhizobium bacteria infect the root hairs and build nodules. Inside those
> nodules the bacteria convert nitrogen gas into a form the plant can use.
> Mycorrhizal fungi have to be present for the partnership to work properly.
>
> The nitrogen goes into the bean plant. It is paying for it.

**4. What is actually happening, part two**
> Almost none of it leaks out to the neighbours while the bean is alive.
> It reaches the rest of the bed when the bean plant dies and decomposes.
>
> And even then it does not go straight into a tomato root. Bacteria and fungi
> break the residue down and hold the nitrogen in their own bodies. Protozoa and
> nematodes graze on them, and the surplus is released at the root, in the form
> a plant can take up.
>
> That is the actual delivery mechanism, and it takes a season.

**5. The part that makes it worse**
> If the bed is already high in available nitrogen, the legume does not bother
> nodulating. Fertilise the beans and you switch the fixing off.

**6. What to do instead**
> Grow the legume, then feed the bed with it. Chop it in at the end of the
> season and leave the roots. This year's beans feed next year's tomatoes.
>
> And feed the organisms that do the delivery. Without protozoa and nematodes
> grazing, the nitrogen stays locked in microbial bodies and nothing reaches the
> root.

**7. The ask**
> Save this for next spring, when the companion planting charts come out again.
