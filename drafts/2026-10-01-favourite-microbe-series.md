# Favourite microbe

## The count is ten, not thirteen

The meeting table lists twelve people. **Ten named a microbe. Two did not:**
Loide Vasquez and Elena Kalli. So this is a ten post series until those two
answer, and the request email below asks them.

## The order, and why Sammie goes first

**1. Sammie Bass, tardigrades.** First because of the dispute, not despite it.
From the notes: she called it debatable herself, Allison and Lukas both wanted
it, she has one as her Google avatar, and the group treated that as establishing
prior claim. She offered to share.

That is a post. A diagram of a tardigrade is not.

**2. Allison Duck, tardigrades and then a backup, both taken.** Runs second so
the joke lands. She lost tardigrades to Sammie and her backup to Carla.

**3. Carla Portugal, amoebae.** For the diversity in the round. Referred to a
"kissing amoeba" and said she has to find a new one after someone claimed hers.

**4. Wes Sander, a large testate amoeba.** Likes that you can see what it has
eaten inside it. *The notes record this as heard: "cellastite", likely a testate
amoeba. Ask Wes what he actually said before publishing a species.*

**5. Ayşen Ustunay, flagellates.** Loves seeing them as babies, running around.
The first organisms to start the soil food web.

**6. Gerald Ramirez, fungi.** Loves a good ratio.

**7. Casey Williams, rotifers.** Remembered late and posted it in chat after his
turn, which is worth keeping in the caption.

**8. Isadora Schmidt, lichens.** Says she loves all her microbes equally.

**9. Brian Daubenspeck, the small underdog microbes.** Partly because many of
them also live in humans.

**10. Tommy Tepper, the small underdog microbes.** Pair with Brian, or run last.

## Caption, post 1

> Sammie has a tardigrade as her avatar, which the rest of us took as settling it.
>
> We asked everyone their favourite microbe at the all-team meeting, as an icebreaker, and it turned into a territorial dispute. Allison wanted tardigrades. So did Lukas. Sammie had the avatar and therefore, the room decided, prior claim. She offered to share. Allison's backup had already been taken by Carla.
>
> Tardigrades are not really soil microbes in the sense the rest of this series is about. They are micro-animals. They live in the film of water around soil particles and moss, and they survive being dried out, frozen and irradiated in a way nothing else here does. Sammie called her own pick debatable before anyone else could.
>
> Ten people, ten microbes, over the next ten weeks. Nobody outside this organisation knows we do this.
>
