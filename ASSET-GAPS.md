# Asset gaps

What the Foundation's picture library does not contain, and what to shoot before
the India trip rather than after it.

Written 17 September 2026, from a file-by-file audit of `INVENTORY.md` plus
direct inspection of the images in question. Where a filename and its contents
disagree, the contents win and the disagreement is recorded at the bottom.

---

## The finding in one line

**The library is one hemisphere, one season and one half of the work.** It is
northern, it is the growing season, and it is almost entirely the moment soil is
held in a hand rather than the moment anything is done to a field.

Sixty-seven photographs in `assets/photo/`. By my count **three** were taken
outside the growing season, and one of those is a stuck tractor.

**The India gap is the urgent one.** The Foundation runs its first Asian workshop
in Coimbatore from 19 to 30 October and holds no photograph taken anywhere in
Asia. Every India post built so far uses a Mexican or European stand-in and says
so on the card. That is honest and it is also visible.

## Activities

**Held:** compost pile building, microscopy at the bench, classroom teaching,
tree planting, soil sampling, hands holding soil, aerial farm views, one compost
tea clip.

**Not held:**

- **Cover crops. Any cover crop, anywhere.** No daikon radish, no clover, no mix,
  no termination. Searching the inventory for these returns only the boilerplate
  description of the USDA folder.
- **Compost or extract being applied at field scale.** We have people building
  piles and we have nothing of the material going out.
- **Harvest.** Nothing of a crop coming off a field.
- **A soil pit or profile.** No horizons, no depth, no root system in section.
- **Visibly degraded or compacted soil.** See the corrections below; the file
  named for it is not it.
- **A before and after pair** of the same ground from the same camera position.
  This is the single highest-value thing on this list and the hardest to
  retrofit, because it requires going back.
- **One person alone on their own land.** Every human photograph we own is a
  group at an event.
- **Brewing at scale**, greenhouse or polytunnel growing, no-till drilling.

## What to shoot in India, in priority order

Evan, Kavi, Loida and Gerald are there for the month. This is a month of access
to a climate, a cropping system and a set of faces the library has none of.

1. **Marked before-and-after positions.** Drive a stake, photograph the frame,
   photograph it again at the end of the fortnight and again next year. Nothing
   else on this list compounds.
2. **Soil in the hand, Indian soil.** The single most reused shape in our
   library, and we have none of it from Asia.
3. **A soil pit.** One dug profile with a tape in it, photographed square on.
4. **Compost and extract going out**, not just being built.
5. **Individual portraits of participants on their own ground**, with consent and
   handles collected at the same time. The scholarship and Field Notes series
   both run short of these.
6. **A rice or wheat field, and the soil under it.** The science post about
   rice-wheat rotation currently illustrates itself with a fist of English soil.
7. **Anything arid.** Dry season Tamil Nadu is a climate we cannot show.

Shoot everything at full resolution, keep the originals, and record the place and
the date at the time. A photograph without a place and a date cannot carry a
caption, and the caption is the part that makes it evidence.

## Corrections to INVENTORY.md found during this audit

Both of these were descriptions inherited from filenames rather than from looking
at the file. Both have been corrected in `INVENTORY.md`.

1. **`assets/microscopy/fungi-in-under-grape-soil.jpg` is not microscopy.**
   It is a field photograph: a vineyard in leaf, rows receding, with a large pale
   mushroom growing in the mulch in the foreground. It shows a fungal fruiting
   body, not hyphae, and it is in the wrong folder.

2. **`assets/photo/fist-of-dry-soil.jpg` is not dry soil.**
   It is a hand squeezing dark, moist, visibly aggregated soil, with crumb
   structure clearly readable. It is the squeeze test. It is one of the better
   images we hold *for showing good structure*, and it is filed and named as its
   opposite.

**The consequence for content:** we hold **no** microscopy image of fungal hyphae
and **no** field photograph of degraded or compacted soil. Both were requested
for the myth carousel and neither exists.

### What I would need sourced

- **Brightfield microscopy of fungal hyphae**, ideally a run of hyphae across the
  field with a scale, and ideally one bacterial-dominated field beside it for
  contrast. This is the Foundation's signature image type and we cannot currently
  illustrate half of our own mechanism 3.
- **One degraded soil photograph**: compacted, platy, cracked or bare, in the
  hand or in section.
- **A matched pair**: degraded and aggregated, same hand, same light, same day.
  One frame each. It would be used in almost every myth post in the bank.

`assets/microscopy/fungal-spores-in-suspension.jpg` is real brightfield and is
good: a dense field of spores and bacterial rods with at least one nematode in
the upper right and some fine filaments crossing the centre. It is not a hyphae
image and should not be captioned as one.

## What is strong, so nobody re-shoots it

- **The microscopy cut-outs**, `assets/microscopy/cutout-1.png` to `cutout-7.png`.
  Seven organisms on transparency: testate amoeba, amoeba, ciliate, a protozoan,
  and three nematodes. They sit on any colour field and no other soil account has
  anything like them.
- **The compost build sequence** at Wild Ken Hill, particularly
  `wild-ken-hill-img-1492.jpg`, which is a pile going up with the material still
  visible in the cage. Mid-build is rarer and more useful than finished.
- **`harringtons-organic-land-care-york-farms-1-768x1024.jpg`**, a gloved hand
  holding back crop residue to show dark crumb soil underneath. It is the
  clearest "structure" photograph in the library.
